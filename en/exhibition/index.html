The exhibition · A journey in chapters

Step inside. The galaxy awaits just beyond the door.

A sandtrooper rides through the desert town. An AT-AT guards the ice world. And Darth Vader really moves. Over 1,200 m² of museum in Mönchengladbach. Scroll your way through the chapters of your mission.

Chapter 01 · The ice world

First stop: Echo Base on Hoth

This museum is more than a hall full of figures. It’s a journey from planet to planet.

Across more than 1,200 m², the exhibition winds through the entire building, and round every corner there’s a new planet. Things get frostiest on Hoth.

The centrepiece is the huge Hoth diorama, complete with Echo Base, snowspeeders and a snowy landscape. This ice world took six months to build.

Right in the middle stands our AT-AT: built in 1999 for the cinema premiere of the Special Edition, then sent on a world tour as a promotional exhibit, and at home in Mönchengladbach since 2007. Go and stand in front of it: your head comes up to roughly the walker’s knee.

Chapter 02 · Jabba’s palace

An audience with the crime lord of Tatooine

Upstairs, Jabba the Hutt lounges on his throne platform, so mighty that you feel tiny standing next to him. A water pipe, red lamps, henchmen lurking in the half-light: his palace looks as if the audience has only just begun.

His court also includes the cackling jester Salacious Crumb and his most famous trophy: Han Solo in carbonite, guarded by Leia in her bounty hunter disguise.

Chapter 03 · Figures & collectors’ treasures

From life-size to 1:18 scale

More than 50 life-size figures fill the building, and one of them moves all by itself. For fans it’s hallowed ground; for families, one of the most unusual days out with kids in North Rhine-Westphalia.

Darth Vader really moves

Here the Dark Lord is a life-size animatronic: he really moves, red lightsaber firmly in hand. Feel free to stand beside him, just keep clear of the blade.

All in all, you’ll meet more than 50 life-size figures, from the golden protocol droid to the little green master with the glowing sword.

Han Solo in carbonite. And you in his shoes.

The galaxy’s most famous deep-frozen smuggler hangs here on the wall, life-size, guarded by a certain gentleman in black with a red lightsaber.

A few steps further on, you get to settle the oldest fan debate yourself: in the interactive scene “Who shot first?” you take a seat as Han Solo, blaster in hand, with Greedo already sitting opposite. Cameras out: this is the photo of your visit.

Sideshow & Hot Toys statues

1:4 and 1:6 scale, detailed down to the last belt buckle. You can practically hear collectors’ hearts beating faster.

Lightsabers & blasters

Replicas in illuminated display cases, from lightsaber hilts to a limited-edition blaster.

Ships & behind the scenes

Ship models by Master Replicas and Attakus, plus behind-the-scenes dioramas in 1:18 scale. Big-screen magic in miniature.

Chapter 04 · The hangar

Just landed: a life-size starfighter

The newest chapter of the exhibition: a life-size starfighter, parked in a recreated Imperial hangar. No peering through glass: you stand right beside it, like a technician about to start the next shift.

And its counterpart in bricks: the LEGO Star Destroyer, 3.20 metres long and built from 400,000 bricks. Fancy rebuilding it at home? Good luck with the sorting.

Chapter 06 · Hall of Fame

Original film costumes

Walk the red carpet past long display cases filled with original costumes from well-known films. Up close you can see fabrics, seams and details that hardly anyone notices on the big screen.

Take your time here: behind every costume there’s a scene you’ll recognise.

Accessibility

There are four steps at the entrance and a staircase at the start of the exhibition. Lightweight wheelchairs and pushchairs can be carried. After that, around 80% of the exhibition is step-free.

The area with the costume collection and ice world lies beyond two staircases and is not wheelchair accessible.
